Times Internet

Marketing · Events & Community

Events Community Paid Media

Marketing as an Ecosystem

Built a multi-stakeholder go-to-market for TSAC & NBT Mic Drop Madness: paid, partnerships, and people.

The Problem

Events are perhaps one of the hardest products to market.

People don't buy an event for what it is. They buy into what they believe it could become for them. And when different people expect different things from the same event, the challenge becomes even more complex.

During my internship at Times Internet, I worked on properties like Times Study Abroad Conclave (TSAC) and NBT Mic Drop Madness. The challenge wasn't simply generating awareness. A student looked for opportunities, a parent looked for security, and a university looked for visibility. Making all of them find value in the same event required far more than just promotions.

In event marketing, attention is limited, choices are endless, and every campaign is racing against time.

My Contribution

My role sat at the intersection of marketing, partnerships, and audience strategy.

I worked on Meta ad campaigns, competitor analysis, market research, and audience segmentation to make communication more targeted and effective. I also worked on influencer marketing, securing collaborations and barter partnerships to expand the event's reach organically.

Beyond campaigns, I reached out to 100+ stakeholders across institutions, partners, and collaborators, learning that event marketing often happens through conversations as much as campaigns.

Some days involved analyzing dashboards and performance metrics. Others involved exploring sponsorship opportunities, acquisition channels, and promotional strategies across print, digital, and social media.

The Impact

Through integrated promotional efforts across channels, I contributed to generating 400+ registrations for the event. We also built an event's digital presence from scratch, growing its community from 0 to 250+ followers within 15-20 days.

More importantly, I gained exposure to the complete marketing funnel, from audience research and campaign planning to partnerships and performance analysis.

For the first time, marketing stopped feeling like separate functions and started feeling like an ecosystem where every piece influences the other.

What I Took Away

Before this internship, I thought marketing was largely about communication.

Times Internet taught me that great marketing begins with understanding people. The same campaign can mean aspiration to a student, reassurance to a parent, and opportunity to a partner.

I learned that data helps you understand what people do, but empathy helps you understand why they do it.

And perhaps my biggest takeaway was this: you're never really marketing an event. You're creating opportunities for people, ideas, and experiences to come together.

400+ Registrations
250+ Community in 20 days
100+ Stakeholders
02

The Indian Express

B2B Events · Lead Strategy

B2B CXO Outreach Lead Gen

Data, Then Conversation

Five Oracle / IBM / SAS-backed CXO summits in two months. Built the pipeline from scratch.

The Problem

The Indian Express was running high-profile B2B events in collaboration with tech giants like Oracle, IBM, and SAS. These events were designed exclusively for industry leaders and decision-makers. The goal was simple: get the right people in the room. But simple doesn't mean easy.

In the span of just two months, I worked across 5 B2B events, each with its own audience and objectives. Thousands of potential prospects existed across industries, but there was no structured pipeline to identify who actually mattered, how to reach them, and how to make them say yes.

Targeting was scattered, slow, and inefficient.

My Contribution

I started where most marketing problems start: with data. But a spreadsheet full of names doesn't fill a room, so the real work was what came after.

Using Apollo and EasyLeadz, I mapped and filtered 1,000+ prospects into a clean, targeted database. I personally reached out to 50+ CXOs and senior executives through invite emails and conducted 100+ outreach calls to understand their priorities and communicate why the event was worth their time.

These weren't scripted conversations. Every interaction was different, and each one taught me that B2B marketing is as much about listening as it is about pitching.

I was also involved in on-ground execution, ensuring that every moving piece came together seamlessly on event day.

The Impact

Qualified lead identification improved by 30%, making outreach sharper and conversations more relevant.

The events brought together 100+ industry leaders and decision-makers from across organizations. Seeing a room full of attendees after weeks of outreach and conversations made me realize how much work goes into building meaningful professional communities.

Being part of that process as a two-month intern was both humbling and exciting.

What I Took Away

B2B taught me that data and people are two completely different problems, and you have to be good at both.

The tools get you the name. The conversation gets them to show up.

When you're on a call with a CXO, you have 30 seconds before they've decided whether you're worth their time. That pressure teaches you to communicate with a lot more intention.

I didn't expect to learn this at 21, but it's probably one of the most useful things I've picked up so far.

+30% Qualified leads
1,000+ Prospects mapped
100+ CXOs in the room
03

Care Trust

Social Impact · CSR Research

CSR Field Ops Research

Empathy Meets Systems Thinking

Menstrual hygiene awareness + sustainable product strategy across schools and villages.

The Problem

Menstrual hygiene is often seen as a health issue, but while working on the Women Health and Hygiene project at Care Trust, I realized that the challenge is much larger.

Lack of awareness, limited access to hygiene products, and social stigma continue to affect many women and young girls. As I explored the issue further, I also discovered its environmental side, with a large portion of sanitary pads containing non-biodegradable materials.

The challenge wasn't just distributing products. It was finding ways to create awareness while also exploring sustainable solutions that could scale.

My Contribution

My work combined community outreach, research, and CSR activities.

I collaborated with 5+ government schools and assisted in organizing 5+ awareness events focused on menstrual hygiene. This included coordinating volunteers, supporting on-ground execution, and participating in village outreach initiatives to engage directly with communities.

Across these initiatives, I helped distribute 200+ sanitary pads and contributed to outreach efforts that eventually impacted 500+ individuals.

Beyond fieldwork, I worked on CSR data collection and research, identifying companies with turnovers above ₹100 crore across different Noida pin codes and studying their existing CSR initiatives to identify potential opportunities for collaboration.

The project also sparked my interest in sustainable alternatives. Since Care Trust manufactured menstrual cups, I conducted competitor analysis and explored how the product could be scaled to reach a wider audience.

The Impact

The project combined awareness, access, and sustainability into one initiative.

Through school collaborations and village outreach campaigns, we were able to extend menstrual hygiene resources to underserved communities while initiating conversations around a topic that is often considered taboo.

For me, the experience was a reminder that meaningful impact requires more than good intentions. It requires research, execution, partnerships, and solutions that can sustain themselves over time.

What I Took Away

Before this project, menstrual hygiene was a topic I had mostly encountered in reports and discussions.

Working directly with communities changed that perspective. I realized that many important challenges persist not because solutions don't exist, but because awareness and access do not reach everyone equally.

Perhaps my biggest takeaway was this: solving social problems requires both empathy and systems thinking. Lasting impact happens when good intentions are backed by data, execution, and sustainable solutions.

04

Miskaveda

Brand · Social Media

D2C Social Media Content

Built From Zero

Built Miskaveda's digital presence from zero across Instagram, Facebook, and LinkedIn.

The Problem

When I joined Miskaveda, the brand existed, but its digital presence did not.

There was no Instagram page, no Facebook community, and no LinkedIn presence.

In a beauty industry already dominated by established players like Forest Essentials and Kama Ayurveda, standing out as a new brand with limited resources was a challenge in itself.

The problem was not just creating social media accounts. It was building a brand identity from scratch, defining its voice, and ensuring that it resonated with audiences across different age groups.

My Contribution

I started from zero, quite literally.

Over the course of two months, I helped build Miskaveda's presence across Instagram, Facebook, and LinkedIn. Working with a team of 3 to 4 members, I acted as the bridge between the founder and the creative team, translating business goals into content strategy and execution.

Since our target audience ranged from 18 to 45 years, I segmented audiences and built a content strategy around three pillars: educational, relatable, and trending content. We focused heavily on communicating the brand's USP of toxin free Ayurvedic skincare through ingredient led and awareness driven content.

I was involved in writing and reviewing captions, planning content calendars, choosing visual themes, and tracking analytics every week to refine the strategy. I also collaborated with three influencers to drive organic reach and identify what resonated most with the audience.

The Impact

Building from scratch meant that every milestone mattered.

In just two months, we grew the brand's following from 0 to 300 to 400 followers entirely through organic efforts. Weekly analytics revealed that reels consistently generated the highest engagement, helping us focus more on the formats that worked best.

More importantly, we established a consistent digital identity across three platforms, giving the brand a voice, a community, and a strong foundation to build on.

What I Took Away

Building something from nothing teaches you things that maintaining something never can.

This was one of the first times I was trusted to take ownership of key decisions, and that taught me that marketing is as much about judgment as it is about creativity.

Being the bridge between the founder and the creative team showed me that good marketing aligns business goals with consumer needs.

Perhaps my biggest takeaway was this. When resources are limited, clarity becomes your biggest advantage. A clear brand story, consistent communication, and a strong understanding of the audience can create meaningful impact even without large budgets.

Skilled Sapiens

Content · Social Media & Leadership

Content Social Media Leadership

Content That Scales

Led social media strategy and a 10-member content team across two brands.

The Problem

Skilled Sapiens operated in the education and consulting space, catering primarily to students seeking certifications, live projects, and career guidance. Among the clients I worked with was Caplexus Capital, a financial services company where translating complex financial concepts into engaging content required both creativity and clarity.

The challenge was not just creating content. It was creating content that educated, engaged, and resonated with audiences across different platforms. With changing trends and limited attention spans, maintaining consistency while keeping the content fresh and relevant became equally important.

My Contribution

Over the course of two months, I worked on social media strategy and content creation for both Skilled Sapiens and Caplexus Capital, managing content across two social media accounts.

I created creatives for Instagram and LinkedIn, experimenting with multiple formats such as static posts, videos, and more than five vox pop videos that captured students' perspectives on academics and careers. These interactions helped transform everyday conversations into engaging and relatable content.

As my work gained recognition, I was entrusted with leading a team of more than 10 members. I conducted more than five training sessions, reviewed over 50 creatives, followed up on deliverables, and helped team members align their work with client expectations and brand requirements.

Alongside mentoring, I also handled content scheduling and posting, ensuring smooth execution across platforms while balancing my academic responsibilities.

The Impact

What began as an individual contributor role gradually evolved into a leadership opportunity.

My work earned me the responsibility of managing a team of more than 10 members, making this one of my first experiences leading and mentoring others.

During this period, the social media presence of both brands also witnessed follower growth, reinforcing the value of consistent and engaging content.

More importantly, I realized that good content creates engagement, but good leadership creates impact at scale.

What I Took Away

Managing my own work while training and mentoring others was challenging, especially alongside academics, but it taught me the importance of communication, ownership, and accountability.

I realized that leadership is not about having all the answers. It is about creating an environment where people can do their best work while feeling supported and aligned with a common goal.

Perhaps my biggest takeaway was this. Trust is not given with a title. It is earned through consistency, reliability, and results.
